### Section H2: What "launching" on TikTok Shop actually means in 2026

```
A TikTok Shop launch is not a Shopify launch with a TikTok 
profile attached. It is the coordinated activation of four 
systems running in parallel from day one:

1. Shop operations: listings, catalog, pricing, fulfillment, 
   compliance, SPS performance.
2. Creator activation: sample seeding, affiliate recruitment, 
   commission strategy.
3. Content engine: short-form video produced and tested across 
   formats, hooks, and angles.
4. Live commerce: brand-owned and affiliate live sessions that 
   convert at 12-18% higher rates than static product pages.

Brands that try to sequence these (catalog first, creators next 
month, live "later") lose the 90-day window where the TikTok 
algorithm is most willing to test a new brand. WE Marketing 
calls this the cold start window. After 90 days without 
meaningful velocity, the algorithm stops testing the brand for 
free, and acquisition costs roughly double.
```

### Section H2: The 90-day TikTok Shop launch framework

```
Three phases, sequential but overlapping. Each phase has 
explicit goals and explicit failure conditions.
```

#### H3: Phase 1 (Days 1-30): Catalog + cold start seeding

```
Goals:
• Shop fully verified, catalog uploaded, pricing locked.
• 50-100 sample creators activated through affiliate seeding.
• 30+ short-form videos posted across creators.
• First 10-50 organic orders to establish review base.

What "done" looks like:
• Listings have at least 5 product images each, 1 product video.
• Affiliate program is open with a tested commission rate 
  (15-25% for beauty/supplements, 10-20% for apparel).
• Creator briefs are sent in English and Spanish, with hooks, 
  banned-claim list, and 2-3 angle suggestions per product.
• At least one product has a hero video with 100K+ views.

Common Phase 1 failures:
• Treating catalog as a one-time upload rather than a live 
  asset that gets re-tested weekly.
• Sending samples without briefs, then blaming creators for 
  weak content.
• Setting commission rates from a Shopify affiliate baseline 
  (5-10%) instead of TikTok Shop benchmarks (15-25%).
```

#### H3: Phase 2 (Days 31-60): Content engine + early scale

```
Goals:
• Identify the 1-2 hero videos that the algorithm has rewarded 
  with 1M+ organic views.
• Double down with comment-reply follow-ups, duets, and 
  remixes from new creators.
• Begin Spark Ads on the hero videos at 3-5x ROAS minimum.
• Recruit 100-300 new affiliates from creators who have 
  already engaged with the brand organically.

Hero video playbook:
A hero video is not a brand commercial. It is a creator-led, 
problem-solution short with a strong first-3-second hook, a 
clear product demo, and a price tag at the moment of peak 
attention. We have seen the same product fail with 100 
generic creator videos and break out with one well-cast hero. 
The math is power-law, not linear.

Common Phase 2 failures:
• Trying to "find" a hero video instead of engineering one. 
  Hero videos are produced through volume, not luck.
• Pulling Spark Ads budget too early when ROAS is volatile. 
  Wait until ROAS is consistent above 2.5x for at least 7 days 
  before scaling spend.
```

#### H3: Phase 3 (Days 61-90): Live + sustained GMV

```
Goals:
• Brand-owned live commerce running 3-5 sessions per week.
• Top affiliate creators activated for live with co-host 
  format.
• Sustained $X daily GMV (the floor depends on category, but 
  beauty brands at this stage typically see $5K-$15K daily 
  GMV; supplements and tech often higher).
• Shop ranking improving on category leaderboards.

What changes in Phase 3:
This is when the brand stops being a "launch" and becomes a 
shop. Live commerce is the highest-leverage tool here. Top 
LIVE sessions for our beauty partner Medicube converted 2,000+ 
units per SKU in a single session. Our wellness partner went 
from $312K monthly GMV in February to $982K in March by adding 
a structured live cadence on top of the existing affiliate 
engine.

Common Phase 3 failures:
• Treating live as a separate channel instead of an extension 
  of the content engine.
• Scaling ad spend before live infrastructure is ready, which 
  drives traffic to a shop that cannot convert at high volume.
• Cutting affiliate commissions once organic GMV picks up. 
  This is the fastest way to lose creator momentum.
```

### Section H2: Common TikTok Shop launch mistakes to avoid

```
The five most common mistakes we see when brands launch:

1. Launching the catalog before the creator network. The shop 
   goes live, no creators are seeded, and the brand spends 
   30 days waiting for organic traffic that never comes.

2. Setting low affiliate commissions to "test the market." 
   TikTok Shop creators have a clear baseline. Below 15% on 
   beauty or supplements, top-performing creators ignore the 
   product entirely.

3. Treating live commerce as optional. Brands that skip Phase 
   3 live activation typically plateau at 60% of the GMV they 
   would have hit with live.

4. Scaling Spark Ads before the hero video is found. Paid 
   amplification of an undertested creative is the fastest 
   way to burn budget.

5. Choosing a single-language creator pool in the US market. 
   The US TikTok Shop market is segmented by language. A 
   diversified creator network across English, Spanish, and 
   Vietnamese reaches all of it. Single-language launches 
   leave 30-40% of the addressable audience on the table.
```

### Section H2: Frequently asked questions about launching on TikTok Shop

```
Q: How much does it cost to launch a brand on TikTok Shop in 2026?
A: A bare-minimum launch (catalog + 50 creators + light video 
   testing) starts around $15K-$25K in agency fees plus product 
   sample budget. A full 90-day launch including live, ads, and 
   300+ creators typically runs $40K-$80K. TikTok Shop itself 
   takes a 5% commission on sales plus payment processing 
   (~2-3%).

Q: How long does it take to launch a brand on TikTok Shop?
A: 14-30 days with a TikTok Shop Partner Agency. 60-120 days 
   in-house. The variable is creator network access. Brands 
   without an existing creator pool spend most of the timeline 
   recruiting.

Q: What products sell best on TikTok Shop?
A: Beauty, supplements, home, fashion, and tech accessories 
   dominate. Products that demo well in 15-30 seconds and have 
   a clear before/after or problem-solution narrative outperform 
   complex technical products by 3-4x.

Q: Do I need to be on Amazon before launching on TikTok Shop?
A: No. Many of our partners launch TikTok Shop first, use the 
   creator-driven demand as proof of product-market fit, and 
   then expand to Amazon and DTC. The creator content also 
   doubles as performance-creative for Amazon DSP and Meta ads.

Q: Can I launch with just my own content and no creators?
A: Technically yes, practically no. Brand-only content on 
   TikTok Shop converts at roughly 1/4 the rate of creator 
   content. The cold-start window closes in 90 days, and 
   without creator activation, most brands miss it.

Q: What is the minimum number of creators for a TikTok Shop 
   launch?
A: 50-100 in the first 30 days for adequate algorithmic signal. 
   Below 50 and the platform does not get enough variation to 
   identify which content works. Above 300 in month one and 
   most brands lose the ability to brief and review at quality.

Q: Should I run TikTok Shop ads on day one of launch?
A: No. Run ads only after a hero video is identified and 
   organic ROAS is consistent. Day-one ads typically burn 
   budget on creative that is not yet optimized.
```
