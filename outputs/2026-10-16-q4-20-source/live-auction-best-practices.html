LIVE Auction Best Practices and FAQs

10/08/2026

Applies to:United States
Key Takeaways
  • Apply for brand authorization before listing trademarked items.
  • Avoid prohibited marketing phrases like "dupe" or gambling-related terms.
  • Meet dispatch deadlines by shipping orders within 2 business days.
This document outlines the rules and best practices for conducting LIVE auctions on the platform to help sellers and creators run successful and compliant auctions.
Disclaimer: The guidance in this document is for learning and reference only and does not constitute legal advice. If you have questions about the laws and regulations of relevant countries, please consult a lawyer.

I. Eligibility for Auction Access

Eligibility for Auction Access

Reference: LIVE Auction Requirements
Core Rule: Auctions on TikTok Shop are permitted only for categories and formats that TikTok Shop expressly allows, including collectibles and eligible Surprise Sets. Auctions for unsupported categories or formats are prohibited.
All live bidding must be run using TikTok Shop's Countdown Bidding Feature. Any auctions or related activities conducted outside the Countdown Bidding Feature are not allowed. Misuse of the Countdown Bidding Feature is also prohibited.
TikTok Shop prohibits behaviors that undermine platform integrity. Creators engaging in such activities may face enforcement actions at TikTok Shop's discretion. These actions can include, but are not limited to: product removal, restriction of listing rights, loss of access to auction-related features, and account suspension.
Countdown Bidding Feature Eligibility:
For Sellers:
  • Account Health Rating (AHR) > 150
  • Seller is not under Temporary Account Restriction Requirements.
  • Seller does not violate Intellectual Property Policy.
For Creators:
  • Creator Health Rating (CHR) > 150
  • Creator does not engage in potentially manipulative or deceptive activity (see Creator Enforcement Policy).

Temporary Listing Products

A Temporary Listing is a fast way to add products to a LIVE session. These listings are temporary and will not sync to TikTok Shop stores or Shopify. Once the LIVE session ends, the listing is removed. If the same product is featured in a future LIVE session, a new Temporary Listing must be created.
Sellers are responsible for ensuring each Temporary Listing accurately represents the product offered for sale. The following are required:
  • Product listed and sold matches the selected category and sub-category.
  • Product information (title, images, brand, and condition) is accurate and not misleading.
  • Product shown or described during LIVE matches the product in the Temporary Listing.
  • No false or inaccurate information in the listing or associated LIVE content
Eligibility for Temporary Listing Access:
For Sellers:
  • Shop Performance Score (SPS) ≥ 3.5 (SPS = Null will not open for Countdown Bidding)
  • Account Health Rating (AHR) > 150
  • Seller is not under Temporary Account Restriction Requirements.
  • Seller does not violate Intellectual Property Policy.
For Creators:
  • Creator Health Rating (CHR) > 150
  • Creator has not violated more than 6 times in the last 30 days (see Creator Enforcement Policy).

II. Common Violation Scenarios in Auctions

Avoid Violating Gambling Policies

Reference: Gambling Policy

Definition of Gambling

Gambling occurs when users provide money or any other item of value in a game or activity for the chance to win a prize. For example, this includes situations where users pay to spin a wheel for the possibility of winning an item of any value.
Gambling activities are prohibited on TikTok Shop, including in both livestreams and videos, even if you do not engage with viewers or offer a chance to win a prize.
Selling any gambling-related products, services, or paraphernalia on the platform is strictly prohibited. This includes, but is not limited to:

Avoid Gambling-Related Phrasing

  • Do not use gambling-related phrasing or setups in your content, including:

Gambling Activities

  • "Place your bets! Win at the casino here."
  • "Sweepstakes alert! Every $1 spent = 3 entries. 3 lucky winners will win a signed copy of my book."
  • "Get your name into our raffle pool. Purchase now for a chance to get a gift!"
  • "Just $1 to play. Guess the number on the dice to win a prize."
  • Playing claw or bingo games on behalf of viewers.

Randomized Product Selection

  • Using any offline or unofficial randomization tool instead of the Live Giveaway tool
  • "Mystery earrings at $7 each. Get your own blind box."
  • "We have the notebook, tote bag, or fan on the wheel. Let's spin the wheel to see what your gift is."

Purchase-Based Incentives That Feel Like Prizes

  • "The highest-spending customer wins a special grand prize."
  • "First customer wins a free candle with their purchase."
  • "Buyers who spend $20 will be entered into a lucky draw for a prize."

Q: What should I do if I believe I was incorrectly flagged for gambling in Auction?

If you believe a mistake has been made, you can submit an appeal on the enforcement notification page. Currently, there is no standardized format for appeal materials. You may provide supporting documentation such as product information and promotional context descriptions, detailing your appeal reasons.

Surprise Set Requirements

Sellers must ensure that products offered as Surprise Sets meet all of the following requirements:
  • Products must be listed in an eligible category, including Collectibles, Beauty & Personal Care, Toys and Hobbies, Sports and Outdoor Leisure Equipment, Home Supplies, Electronics, or Fashion Accessories
  • Collectibles products may include, but are not limited to, trading cards, comics and manga, or model cars
  • All items in a set must be manufacturer-sealed, in original packaging, and unmodified.
  • All items in a set must come from the same collectibles sub-category (for example, sport trading cards and non-sport trading cards may not be mixed).
  • All items in the set must have similar retail or market value to support fairness.
  • Sellers are prohibited from selling bullions (precious metals like gold or silver in the form of bars, ingots, or coins) in this format.

Surprise Set Pricing Limits

Sellers must comply with the following maximum bidding price limits per Surprise Set:
  • Collectibles: maximum bidding price of $5,000 per set
  • All other eligible categories (Beauty and Personal Care, Toys and Hobbies, Sports and Outdoor Leisure Equipment, Home Supplies, Electronics, Fashion Accessories): maximum bidding price of $500 per set
Products in these categories are subject to applicable category return requirements, which may include Change of Mind returns.

Customer Purchase Limits

The following purchase limits apply to auction-related product transactions, including Surprise Sets:
  • Customers under 25 years old may purchase up to 8 auction-related products per day (including, but not limited to, Surprise Sets, card breaks, lucky scoops, and blind boxes)
  • Cool-off period: Customers who purchase 20 auction-related products will enter a 15-minute cool-off period before additional auction-related purchases can be made. After every additional 20 auction-related purchases, another 15-minute cool-off period will apply

Feature Access Pathways

Eligible sellers may access the Surprise Set feature through the following pathways:
From Seller Center:
  1. Go to Manage Products
  2. Click Add New Product
  3. Select Create Surprise Set
From LIVE Console:
  1. Schedule an event and select Go LIVE
  2. Go to the Surprise Set tab, or
  3. Select Auctions, then click Create Surprise Set

Strictly Prohibit IP Infringement / Selling Counterfeits

Apply for and Submit Brand Authorization

Reference: Requirements for Brand Authorization
Core Rule: You must apply for brand authorization to list trademarked products on TikTok Shop. Listing products without brand authorization may lead to enforcement actions. These actions can include product removal, restriction of listing rights, loss of access to auction features, and account suspension.

Other Questions

Q1: In livestream auction scenarios, what are the compliance red lines for determining intellectual property rights infringement (IPR) and counterfeit violations? How should sellers effectively avoid these risks?Requirements for Brand AuthorizationHow To Avoid Counterfeits, Knockoffs, And Other Trademark Infringement ViolationsHow To Avoid Selling Unoriginal And Inauthentic Products
  1. In livestream auction scenarios, the core red lines for IPR and Counterfeit are the same as for ordinary product sales, and they are not relaxed because an auction format is used. Sellers should pay particular attention to the following points:
  • When selling branded products, sellers should ensure that the product source is legal and authentic, and retain proof such as purchase records and authorization documents so they can provide them when required by the platform
  • Sellers must not sell counterfeit products, including "factory A-grade goods," "original order goods," "super A-grade goods," or other products manufactured without brand authorization
  • Sellers must not mislead consumers through product titles, livestream verbal claims, cover images, listed product information, or other means into believing that the product has an official authorization, partnership, or affiliation with the brand
  • Even if the product itself is genuine, issues with product information display, promotional wording, or supporting materials may still create IPR risk
In short, livestream auctions are not a special exemption scenario. The platform will conduct a comprehensive assessment based on factors such as product authenticity, sourcing, product presentation, and the supporting materials submitted by the seller.Sellers are advised to prepare and retain sourcing records and supporting documents in advance during operations, and avoid promotional wording that may easily cause consumer confusion or misunderstanding.Q2.When selling well-known branded products, under what circumstances must a brand authorization letter be provided? If a seller sells compliant clearance inventory purchased through legitimate channels but has not yet obtained direct authorization, how should the seller prevent infringement risk?At present, livestream auctions of major branded products require either brand authorization or a purchase invoice. If a seller sells branded products without direct brand authorization, it is recommended that the seller (1) avoid promotional wording that may lead consumers to believe there is an official authorization, partnership, or special relationship, and (2) retain sourcing and supporting materials in advance to reduce the risk of subsequent penalties and to support timely appeals.
Q3. What are the different applicable scenarios for brand authorization letters and purchase invoices in compliance review?Purchase invoices and authorization documents are primarily used to prove that the product source is legal and authentic, but they apply to different scenarios. If the products being sold are third-party branded products, the relevant brand information should be recorded truthfully.
  • In principle, sellers should provide brand authorization to prove that they have the right to sell the products. Brand authorization has the strongest evidentiary value because the seller has obtained direct or indirect authorization from the brand. Sellers may even complete brand authorization certification in the system in advance to avoid erroneous enforcement by the platform’s main control system and to facilitate future appeals.
  • If a seller sells third-party branded products through purchase and resale, the seller is advised to properly retain purchase evidence such as receipts. If the seller later faces a brand or platform complaint/penalty for trademark infringement: in the United States, the seller may rely on the "trademark exhaustion doctrine" (a legal principle that allows the resale of trademarked products after the trademark owner has sold them) and appeal by proving that the products were obtained from legitimate sources and resold in their original condition. When appealing, the seller should provide original purchase evidence such as receipts for the disputed branded products.
Q4. Does selling products manufactured by an Original Equipment Manufacturer (OEM) factory without official authorization, such as so-called "factory A-grade goods," constitute a counterfeit violation?Yes. "Factory A-grade goods," "original order goods," "super A-grade goods," and other products manufactured without brand authorization may generally be deemed counterfeit-risk products, even if they are produced by the brand’s OEM factory, use the same raw materials, or are close in quality to genuine products. When determining whether a product is genuine, the platform typically focuses on whether the product was manufactured with authorization from the brand rights holder and entered lawful sales channels through authorization or legal transfer, rather than relying only on product quality, production location, or similarity to genuine products. Therefore, sellers should avoid selling or promoting such products, and should ensure that the products they sell come from legal and traceable sources while retaining relevant supporting materials to reduce operational risk.
Q5. Why might different sellers receive different violation determinations when selling similar products?Selling similar products does not mean that the same determination will be reached. The platform usually conducts a comprehensive assessment based on factors such as product source, authorization status, how product information is presented, the branded materials used, and the supporting documents the seller can provide. For example, when selling products from the same brand, some sellers may be able to prove that the products came from lawful distribution channels or have complete supporting materials such as purchase receipts and authorization documents. Other sellers may be unable to prove the product source, or may use wording in titles, images, livestream verbal claims, or other promotions that could easily lead consumers to believe there is an official authorization or partnership, thereby creating IP infringement risk.
Q6. If a seller receives an intellectual property rights infringement (IPR) or counterfeit violation notice, how should the seller respond properly and prevent issues in advance?After receiving an IPR or Counterfeit penalty, sellers are advised to first carefully review the penalty notice to understand the specific violation reason, the products involved, and the appeal requirements. If the seller believes the penalty was issued in error, the seller may promptly file an appeal by submitting genuine, complete, and valid supporting materials as required by the platform, such as:
  • Purchase invoices
  • Authorization documents
  • Logistics records
From a prevention perspective, sellers are advised to focus on the following in daily operations:
  • Apply for brand authorization in Seller Center
  • Ensure that product sources are legal and authentic
  • Properly retain supporting materials such as purchase, authorization, and logistics records
  • Avoid selling high-risk products such as "factory A-grade goods," "original order goods," and "super A-grade goods"
  • Use brand trademarks, official images, celebrity likenesses, and promotional wording that may mislead consumers with caution
  • Conduct regular self-checks, promptly delist products with potential risks, and establish a complete product traceability and record-retention mechanism in advance

III. Optimize Shop Operation Quality, Improve Shop Experience, and Reduce Negative Product Feedback

Improve Shop Performance Score

Reference: Guide to Shop Performance Score (SPS)
Core Concept: Shop Performance Score (SPS) is a dynamic 0-5 rating system. You will automatically receive a Shop Performance Score when you have delivered at least 30 orders in the past 90 calendar days. SPS focuses on three key areas: Product Satisfaction, Fulfillment & Logistics, and Customer Service.

Shop Performance Score Metric Breakdown

Product Satisfaction:
Negative Review Rate (NRR): The percentage of delivered orders in the last 60 days that received an initial 1- or 2-star product review
Non-Buyer Fault Return and Refund Rate (NBFR): The percentage of delivered orders in the last 60 days that led to a refund or return request caused by the seller or logistics partner
Fulfillment & Logistics:
Seller Fault Cancellation Rate (SFCR): The percentage of orders placed in the past 30 days that were canceled due to the seller's fault
On-Time Delivery Rate (OTDR): The percentage of orders delivered by their designated "deliver-by date" from the total orders expected to be delivered within the last 30 days
Customer Service:
IM Dissatisfaction Rate: The percentage of chat conversations in the past 60 days where customers gave a low satisfaction rating (1-2 stars)
After-Sales Handling Time: The average time sellers take to handle refund, return, cancellation, or replacement requests in the past 60 days

Improve Negative Product Feedback - Voice of Customer (VoC)

Reference: Guide to VoC (Voice of Customer) & VoC Case Study

VoC Introduction

Core Value: Voice of Customer (VoC) is a product insight tool in Seller Center that helps you analyze customer feedback and better understand product experience performance. Access: Seller Center > Account Health > Voice of Customer
VoC categorizes products into three levels:
VoC classifies products into At Risk, Needs Attention, and Good levels based on their performance relative to the category average.
  • At Risk: Your product is performing significantly below the category average
  • Needs Attention: Your product is performing below the category average
  • Good: Your product is performing in line with or better than the category average
We recommend prioritizing products marked At Risk and Needs Attention. These products may indicate elevated product experience risk and may result in potential platform enforcement, such as product removal or restriction of listing rights. Timely review and improvement can help address potential issues and improve performance.
Under Product Insights, click At Risk, Needs Attention, or Good next to "Products with VoC" to view the corresponding products in the list below.

Fulfillment Management - Dispatch on Time, Reduce Late Dispatch Rate (LDR)

Reference: Late Dispatch Rate Requirements
Core Rule: Late Dispatch Rate (LDR) represents the percentage of dispatched orders that were not scanned by carriers within the required dispatch Service Level Agreement (SLA). Sellers must ensure orders are picked up and scanned by carriers within 2 business days so that the order status changes to "In Transit." The platform recommends LDR ≤ 4%. If LDR > 10%, enforcement actions may be applied, including but not limited to restriction of listing rights or loss of access to platform features.

How is LDR Calculated?

LDR for orders created during the evaluation period = Number of non-Fulfilled by TikTok (FBT) orders dispatched later than the dispatch SLA / Total number of orders that should have been dispatched within the dispatch SLA (including FBT orders).

LDR Appeal Scenarios and Requirements

For LDR violations, TikTok Shop will only consider appeals for the following reasons:
  1. Orders were scanned by carriers on time: The carrier scanned the order within the dispatch timeframe, but Seller Center did not record it.
  2. Logistics Service Provider liability: The order was late because:
  • The warehouse was slow to dispatch the order
  • The carrier was slow to do the first scan
  1. Uncontrollable events: Orders were late because of events you could not prevent, such as:
  • Public emergencies
  • Natural disasters
  • Unexpected logistics disruptions
  1. TikTok Shop technical issues: Order status updates were late because of Seller Center issues, including but not limited to:
  • Wrong dispatch deadlines shown in Seller Center
  • Order statuses not synced to Seller Center
  1. ERP or ISV system issues: Order status was not updated on time because of problems with your Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) or Independent Software Vendor (ISV) system provider (for sellers using MCF, Shopify, Rithum, and so on).
  2. Customer-related delays: Orders were late because of customer requests or wrong customer information, such as:
  • Wrong customer information
  • Customer asking for delayed shipment
  • Not being able to contact the customer
Appeal Time Limit: The initial appeal must be submitted within 30 days of the enforcement message. If the first appeal is unsuccessful, the second appeal must be submitted within 15 days of initial appeal rejection. Each enforcement can only be appealed 2 times.

IV. After-Sales Policy: Final Sale

Auction After-sales webinar: Learn How to Boost Sales | TikTok Shop Academy | United States

Auction Final Sale

Auction orders are final sales. Buyers cannot return auction items due to a change of mind.
For valid issues (such as items that are missing, damaged, not received, or not as described), buyers may request a return or refund within 14 days of delivery and must provide evidence.

Cancellation Rules for Auction Orders

Buyers cannot directly cancel auction orders before the seller ships the order.
If a buyer would like to request cancellation before shipment, they can contact Customer Service or message the seller. Cancellation requests submitted before shipment will be reviewed by the seller. Customer Service may also assist with cancellation requests for reasons such as accidental purchase or unauthorized purchase.
If a cancellation request is submitted after the seller ships the order, it will be handled according to TikTok Shop’s current cancellation policy.

Auction Protection

Auction Protection is a platform-level safeguard for auction orders. It gives buyers a clear path to support for valid seller-fault issues while preserving the finality of auction wins.
Covered issues:
  • Item not received
  • Item arrived damaged or defective
  • Item not as described or shown during the live session
  • Missing items
Not covered:
  • Change-of-mind reasons (buying by mistake, no longer needed, finding a better price)
