How to Manage Your Invalid Seller Rejection Rate (ISRR)

09/22/2026

Applies to:United States
Returns & Refunds PolicyReturns & Refunds Management
Key Points:
  • ISRR looks at eligible after-sales requests that your shop reviewed and rejected, but were overturned by the platform after customer appeal.
  • Sellers should maintain an ISRR of under 20%.
  • Keeping ISRR low helps you reduce disputes, avoid extra platform intervention, and create a smoother experience for customers.
  • The goal of ISRR is to make sure that after-sales requests are handled fairly for both seller and customer.
Invalid Seller Rejection Rate (ISRR) shows the percentage of eligible seller-reviewed after-sales requests in which your rejection is later determined to be invalid. Invalid outcomes include customer-supported arbitration decisions and unsuccessful seller appeals.
A lower ISRR indicates that more of your initial after-sales decisions are upheld.

What ISRR measures

ISRR looks at eligible after-sales requests that your shop reviewed and rejected and that were later determined to be invalid. Invalid outcomes include customer-supported arbitration decisions and unsuccessful seller appeals.

How is ISRR calculated

Your ISRR is the number of eligible invalid seller rejections divided by the number of eligible seller-reviewed after-sales requests, multiplied by 100. It is calculated over a rolling 8-week window and evaluated weekly.
NOTE: ISRR applies only when both the numerator and denominator are greater than 10.

Why ISRR matters for your business growth

Maintaining a low ISRR isn't just about meeting policy requirements, it's about building stronger customer relationships to drive long-term growth for your shop. Sellers with lower rejection rates experience:
  • Higher repeat purchases
  • Significantly higher customer satisfaction scores
  • Stronger trust in your shop reputation
  • More positive reviews and recommendations

What is the ISRR requirement

Sellers are required to maintain an ISRR under 20%. An ISRR of 20% or higher can trigger a warning. For Refund-only and Return and Refund requests, ISSR must be under 30%.
See Requirements for Invalid Seller Rejection Rate (ISRR) Metric for more details.

How to monitor your ISRR

You can track your ISRR performance through multiple ways:
  1. Seller Center Banner: Notifications about your current status
  2. Analytics > Post Purchase > Return & Refund: View your ISRR calculation
  3. Historical Data: See your performance over the past 9 weeks with interactive charts

How to appeal platform

Even during platform-managed review periods, you can still appeal approved after-sales requests with valid supporting evidence. While you cannot appeal your ISRR metric directly, you can appeal individual after-sales decisions. Here's how:
  1. In Seller Center, go to Orders > Manage Returns.
  2. Use the 'Can Be Appealed' filter or click the 'Appeal' tag.
  3. Submit your appeal within 30 calendar days with supporting evidence.
After clicking 'Appeal', you can submit relevant reasons for your appeal, upload supporting evidence, or provide additional comments to our Customer Service team to assist with the appeal process.

Tools to make after-sales management easier

TikTok Shop offers seller flexibility tools such as auto-approvals for returns and refund-only requests. With these tools, you can "set it and forget it", reducing your time spent on after-sales management and minimizing the risk of non-compliance.
Benefits of Auto-Approval:
  • Reduces manual workload
  • Speeds up resolution times
  • Helps maintain good ISRR performance
  • Counts toward your metrics (unlike platform auto-approvals)
How to Configure Auto-Approval:
  1. In Seller Center, go to Orders > Fulfillment Settings
  2. Select the Returns and Refunds tab
  3. Create rules for Returnless Refund, Auto-Approval Refunds, Replacements, or Exchanges
  4. Set criteria based on price, category, or time period
Learn more about Auto-Approval features in the Customizing Your Return and Refund Features guide.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Invalid Seller Rejection Rate (ISRR)?
ISRR measures the percentage of aftersales requests that were rejected by sellers and later found to be valid in favor of buyers after review or arbitration. A higher ISRR indicates opportunities to improve decision-making during the initial review stage.
What is a good ISRR?
Sellers want to maintain an ISRR of under 20%. Ideally, you want the lowest ISRR possible because it signifies a consistent after-sales experience that supports customer expectations.
What happens if my ISRR is higher than 20%?
Sellers will get a warning and be provided with additional education if their ISRR exceeds 20%. If there are persistent issues with a seller's ISRR, then the seller will have mandated platform-review for certain after-sales requests.
During this time, you will continue to review each request first, but if they reject an after-sales request, the platform will review the rejection decision.
Where can I see my after-sales requests?
You can find all aftersales requests in Seller Center by navigating to Orders > Manage Returns. You will be able to view key details, such as aftersales request types, as well as the initiation and completion time of each request.
Where can I configure auto-approval rules?
To improve your aftersales performance, you can configure auto approval rules through Seller Center by navigating to Orders > Return Settings > "Cancellations" or "Returns & Refunds" These settings allow you to enable automatic approval for certain aftersales requests based on predefined criteria or timeframe. By using these features, you can reduce manual intervention, speed up resolution times, and increase your Self-Service Aftersale Rate & Processing Time, which contributes to better SPS and faster performance.
